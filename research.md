## Research

My research focuses on combining energy networks with novel 'smart' technologies and processes (particularly power electronics-based 'inverter based resources'), aiming to maximise utilization of each of those to increase system efficiency.

* Increasing utilization of an energy network through smart technologies means finding new ways of operating smart technologies to increase network capacity. Increasing network capacity is required to minimize requirements for costly, disruptive and unpopular network reniforcements.
* Increased utilization of smart technologies within an energy networks means finding new ways of designing, co-ordinating or connecting devices to maximise their usefulness within the system. Increased utilization can increase efficiency of the grid, reduces renewables curtailment, therefore ultimately reducing operational costs of the system.

In my RAEng Research Fellowship, I am exploring this in the context of novel hybrid systems, i.e., systems for which there is both a discrete (on-off) and continuous (smooth) characteristic. These characteristics arise when combining traditional power system technologies (e.g., network reconfiguration; protection; bang-bang/hysteresis control) with 'smart' power system technologies (e.g., network operator owned power electronics; low carbon technologies such as solar PV and electric vehicles). Combining these in novel ways can result in interesting and unexpected system behaviour and result in much more efficient utilization of both within the energy system.


My academic publications list is my [Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Gkxd78QAAAAJ&hl=en) page. Tools and data are available from [NU figshare](https://data.ncl.ac.uk/authors/Matthew_Deakin/11948024) and [Github](https://github.com/deakinmt).

#### Active initiative: a new data specification for distribution system (optimal) power flow

We have developed and a new data specification for unbalanced (multiconductor) power flow in electrical distribution grids and are presently revising this to reach parity with the industry-standard OpenDSS tool (as part of the IEEE Task Force on Benchmarking Multiconductor OPF for Distribution Systems). For more information or if you are interested to contribute please see the [data specification][bmopf_data_spec] and the [distribution-system-opt][dsopt] site.

### Recent outputs


**Power electronic devices for network operators**

Network operator-owned power electronic devices (PEDs) have been used in transmission networks for several decades, but are yet to be exploited by distribution network operators. I am interested in combining network reconfiguration and power electronics to enable more flexible operation that can maximise the use of utilization of both.

**Analytics for Planning and Decision-Making**

Uncertainty is increasing the complexity of energy systems at all scales, from gigawatt-scale data centres to new customer energy resources such as plug-in solar. These motivate new analytic methods to enable an efficient, secure and well-utilized energy system, particularly given the new rapid software and analytics development pipelines newly available as a result of AI tools.

**Benchmarking, Tools and Data**

Power distribution systems are transitioning from largely passive, 'fit-and-forget' planning and operation strategies to active, responsive distribution systems. The requirements and scale of the models, data and tools required used for those activities is therefore undergoing a transformation. As well as the aforementioned [IEEE Task Force Data Specification][bmopf_data_spec], I have published a number of open tools and resources to support anaylsis and research.

* **Deakin, M.**, Vanin, M., Fan, Z. and Van Hertem, D., 2024. Smart energy network digital twins: Findings from a UK-based demonstrator project. International Journal Of Electrical Power & Energy Systems, 162, p.110302. [DOI][dtwin] / [Preprint][dtwin_pre]
* **Deakin, M.**, Greenwood, D., Walker, S. and Taylor, P.C., 2021, June. Hybrid European MV–LV network models for smart distribution network modelling. In 2021 IEEE Madrid PowerTech (pp. 1-6). IEEE. [DOI][mvlv] / [Preprint][mvlv_pre]
* Morstyn, T., Collett, K.A., Vijay, A., **Deakin, M.**, Wheeler, S., Bhagavathy, S.M., Fele, F. and McCulloch, M.D., 2020. OPEN: An open-source platform for developing smart local energy system applications. Applied Energy, 275, p.115397. [DOI][open] / [Preprint][open_pre]

Tools and data for these and other projects are available from [NU figshare](https://data.ncl.ac.uk/authors/Matthew_Deakin/11948024) and [Github](https://github.com/deakinmt).
